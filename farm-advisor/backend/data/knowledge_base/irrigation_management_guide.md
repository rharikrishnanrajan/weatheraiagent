# Irrigation Management and Soil Moisture Scheduling Guide

## Core Principles of Agronomic Water Management
Efficient irrigation balances crop water consumption (Crop Evapotranspiration, $ET_c$) with natural precipitation and soil moisture holding capacity. Over-irrigation causes nutrient leaching, root hypoxia, and pathogen multiplication; under-irrigation causes stomatal closure and yield reductions.

## Soil Water Dynamics by Texture
1. **Sandy Soils:** Available Water Capacity (AWC) is low (25-35 mm/m). Water percolates rapidly beyond the root zone. 
   - *Strategy:* High frequency, low volume applications (e.g. daily or alternate day drip fertigation). Do not exceed 12-15 mm per irrigation event.
2. **Loam & Silt Loam Soils:** AWC is high (60-80 mm/m). Excellent capillary action and aeration.
   - *Strategy:* Moderate frequency (every 3 to 5 days). Can absorb 20-30 mm per application without runoff.
3. **Clay Soils:** Total water retention is high (80-100 mm/m), but infiltration rates are slow (< 5 mm/hour).
   - *Strategy:* Pulse irrigation (e.g. 1 hour ON, 1 hour OFF) to prevent surface puddling, oxygen depletion, and crusting.

## Critical Growth Stages for Moisture Stress
- **Tomatoes:** Flowering and fruit expansion. Water deficits during early fruit set cause blossom-end rot (calcium deficiency aggravated by uneven watering). Drought during ripening causes fruit cracking when water is suddenly reintroduced.
- **Corn / Maize:** Tasseling and silking (reproductive window). Severe moisture stress during pollination can decrease grain yield by up to 40-50% due to pollen desiccation and delayed silk emergence.
- **Wheat:** Crown root initiation (20-25 days after sowing), heading/flowering, and milk stage.
- **Soybeans:** Pod development and seed filling (R3 to R5 stages).

## Weather Integration Rules
1. **Forecast Rule:** If more than 15 mm of rain is forecasted with > 70% probability in the next 48 hours, suspend planned irrigation.
2. **Evaporation Timing:** Never apply overhead sprinkler irrigation during peak midday solar radiation (11:00 AM - 3:00 PM). Up to 30-40% of applied water is lost to direct evaporation and wind drift. Prefer drip or early morning (5:00 AM - 8:30 AM) irrigation.
3. **Leaching Fraction:** In arid or saline groundwater regions, apply an additional 10-15% seasonal leaching fraction to flush accumulated salts below the root zone.

Source: FAO Irrigation and Drainage Paper 56 - Crop Evapotranspiration.
