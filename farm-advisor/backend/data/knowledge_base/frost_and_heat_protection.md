# Extreme Weather Protection: Frost, Heatwave & Storm Defense

## 1. Frost Protection Strategies

### Types of Frost
- **Radiation Frost:** Clear, calm skies (< 5 km/h wind) and rapid radiative cooling of ground heat overnight. Thermal inversion layer forms (air at canopy height is colder than air 10m above).
- **Advective Freeze:** Cold, turbulent arctic air mass moves into the area accompanied by strong winds (> 15 km/h) and low humidity. Covers and water methods are less effective.

### Defensive Countermeasures
1. **Soil Hydration (Pre-Frost Irrigation):**
   - Moisten bare, firm soil 24 to 48 hours prior to an anticipated radiation frost. Wet soil conducts heat 3-4 times faster and stores twice the solar thermal energy of dry, loose soil, radiating warmth upward throughout the night.
2. **Row Covers & Agribon Fleece:**
   - Lightweight polypropylene fabric (17 to 30 g/m²) can elevate canopy temperatures by 2°C to 4°C. Ensure fabric is anchored to the soil so heat does not escape around margins.
3. **Overhead Sprinkler Frost Protection:**
   - Sprinklers must be turned on when wet-bulb temperature drops to 0.5°C and kept running continuously until ice begins melting in morning sunlight. The latent heat of fusion (80 calories per gram of water freezing into ice) protects plant tissue at 0°C. **Warning:** If water delivery stops while freezing wind continues, evaporative cooling will destroy the crop.

## 2. Heatwave & Extreme Solar Radiation Mitigation
- **Thermal Stress Limits:** Temperatures above 35°C (95°F) cause stomatal shutdown, reduced photosynthetic assimilation, and pollen sterility in tomatoes, peppers, and beans.
- **Countermeasures:**
  1. **Shade Nets (30% to 50% Black or Aluminet):** Reduces leaf surface temperature by 3°C to 6°C.
  2. **Kaolin Clay Spray:** Reflective mineral particle barrier (Surround WP) applied to foliage blocks excess UV radiation, reduces sunburn damage on fruits, and suppresses transpiration stress.
  3. **Early Morning Deep Hydration:** Water root zones between 4:00 AM and 7:00 AM so plants enter peak afternoon heat with full turgor pressure.

## 3. Heavy Rain & Strong Wind Lodging Defense
- Clean field drainage furrows and unblock retention ponds before heavy storm fronts (> 25 mm).
- For tall crops (corn, sorghum, indeterminate tomatoes), ensure adequate potassium and silicon nutrition which thickens stem culm walls and reduces mechanical lodging risks.
- Cease all heavy tractor field passes when soil moisture is above field capacity to prevent severe subsoil compaction.

Source: Agricultural Climate Risk Management Manual.
